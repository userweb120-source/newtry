A few facts

The story

It started downstairs

I grew up in a working-class neighborhood in Cairo, in a family that worked in trade and manufacturing. There was a workshop downstairs from our home.

By the time I was around 16, I was already working with my father and uncles: buying raw plastic, following mold-making work, standing next to machines during testing and production, and eventually learning to run some of the machines myself. I also distributed products to traders and worked in sales and trade.

Before I knew what ERP meant, I already knew what raw materials, machines, stock, customers, suppliers, cash in, and cash out looked like in real life.

Then I found the internet

Back when the internet meant dial-up, and my computer had 64 MB of RAM before I proudly upgraded it to 128 MB, my friends cared about games. I was stuck on something else: what was that handshake and the number that appeared when the connection started? So I started looking.

One small question opened an entire world. I learned domains, hosting, and website building, and yes, I used FrontPage too. Then I stumbled into open source and free software. That changed more than how I used software. It changed how I thought: open things up, understand them, question why they’re built that way, and don’t assume the current way is the only way.

I went deeper into Linux, working with CentOS, Fedora, openSUSE, and Ubuntu, and started dealing directly with servers. Because I already had a commercial background, turning that knowledge into business felt natural. I started selling domains and hosting, then website design and development, while still working in trade and manufacturing and trying to study at the same time.

Education

After preparatory school, I went to commercial secondary school, then joined a Management Information Systems institute. I cared about accounting, management, and the subjects I could actually use, but I was never interested in being a model student.

Part of the curriculum felt disconnected from the world I was already learning and working in. I learned what felt useful, but I didn’t finish university. I don’t treat that as an achievement, and I don’t treat it as something to hide. It simply happened. Self-learning and work took a bigger share of my attention.

The journey

Helping people start

I helped entrepreneurs get businesses off the ground: choosing the right tools, analyzing markets, working through business plans and Business Model Canvas, and turning a problem or idea into something that could actually be tested. I also trained young people across different Egyptian governorates on innovation and business kickoff.

YouTube

I’ve had a YouTube channel since 2020. The content has passed 4 million views, and the channel has grown beyond 10,000 subscribers. I don’t see that as a badge I need to prove anything with. It’s simply part of the public trail I’ve built over the years.

What I’m running now

I don’t see these projects as isolated islands. Every one of them carries something from the one before it, and each one taught me something I used in the next.

So what do I actually do?

If you ask me what I do, the answer usually needs more than one job title. On the same day, I might be understanding a process inside a factory, fixing an ERP problem, reviewing a server, thinking through a product, or discussing a business model.

I get bored by routine very quickly. My work happens to fit that flaw pretty well. The real work is understanding the problem properly and choosing the right way to solve it. Sometimes that’s software. Sometimes infrastructure. Sometimes ERP. And sometimes the right answer is not to build anything at all.

Off
